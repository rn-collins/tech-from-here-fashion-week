## Governing question

What survives when fashion memory is split across institutions and commercial platforms?

## Canonical essay

Shanghai compresses schedule, factory-city, social image, cloud runway, buyer order, product identity and distributed memory into synchronized platform time. This chapter follows memory: The venue disappears, schedules change and links decay; preservation choices determine which technical history remains citable and contestable.

Donghua University’s textile-and-costume museum and the Shanghai Municipal Archives expose different institutional routes into material and civic memory. That earlier record fixes a point in time; it does not prove an inevitable progression toward the present.

An archive is not the residue of the week. It is infrastructure that decides which claims, credits and absences remain available for argument.

Organizer news, university collections, archives, scholarship databases and commercial platforms preserve different fragments under different access and reuse rules. The claim remains attached to its named source because public visibility is evidence of publication—not independent proof of every underlying practice.

Operationally, the chain runs through the organizer, photographer, institution, web archivist, rights holder and researcher. Each controls a different record or decision, while the finished show encourages us to experience those decisions as a single seamless event.

Shanghai’s governing verb is synchronization. It is an editorial lens, not a claim about the city’s essence: it helps test how calendars, credentials, media, labor records, commercial data and archives become legible here.

No complete public archive was located that preserves every Shanghai show’s invitations, worker credits, venue plans, source files, rights, platform metrics and commercial outcomes. A future plate is therefore a documented trajectory or an explicit open question—not a forecast manufactured from a press release.

This public record cannot establish what may remain private, paywalled, undigitized, untranslated or held at show level. That limit belongs in the published account because an evidentiary blank is not evidence of absence.
