## Governing question

Which objects, interfaces and technical credits enter public memory?

## Canonical essay

Paris exposes the technical production of legitimacy through calendar inclusion, monumental rooms, official images, digital programmes, showrooms and archives. This chapter follows memory: The venue disappears, schedules change and links decay; preservation choices determine which technical history remains citable and contestable.

Palais Galliera, Paris Musées and Gallica expose different public memory systems: museum objects, open-image filters and digitized national collections. That earlier record fixes a point in time; it does not prove an inevitable progression toward the present.

An archive is not the residue of the week. It is infrastructure that decides which claims, credits and absences remain available for argument.

Discovery access and reuse permission are separate: Paris Musées marks some public-domain reproductions, while collection and library records require item-level checking. The claim remains attached to its named source because public visibility is evidence of publication—not independent proof of every underlying practice.

Operationally, the chain runs through the organizer, photographer, institution, web archivist, rights holder and researcher. Each controls a different record or decision, while the finished show encourages us to experience those decisions as a single seamless event.

Paris’s governing verb is legitimacy. It is an editorial lens, not a claim about the city’s essence: it helps test how calendars, credentials, media, labor records, commercial data and archives become legible here.

No complete public archive was located that preserves every Paris show’s invitations, worker credits, venue plans, source files, rights and commercial outcomes. A future plate is therefore a documented trajectory or an explicit open question—not a forecast manufactured from a press release.

This public record cannot establish what may remain private, paywalled, undigitized, untranslated or held at show level. That limit belongs in the published account because an evidentiary blank is not evidence of absence.
