Comparative field note · seven editions

The same week does not exist twice.

This comparison derives from the seven city evidence editions. It compares public infrastructures—not the quality, creativity or importance of their designers.

London / relay

Programme becomes transmission.

The strongest public record joins schedule, commissioned moving image, livestream and procurement evidence. Its limit is item-level operational adoption.

New York / clock

Calendar becomes coordination.

A distributed venue and production ecology is made legible through time, credential and industry interfaces. Worker-level data remains uneven.

Milano / inheritance

House codes meet operating systems.

Trademark, palazzo, craft and showroom systems expose how historic authority travels through contemporary commerce.

Paris / legitimacy

The calendar acts as a code.

Official inclusion, monumental staging, federation media and showroom networks separate visibility from institutional recognition.

Shanghai / synchronization

Platform time joins order time.

Schedule, social circulation, cloud runway and showroom systems compress image, attention and transaction—without proving one universal stack.

Copenhagen / verification

Participation meets evidence.

Minimum Standards, review, teardown duties and announced screening tools make the evidentiary threshold unusually explicit while stopping short of certification.

Tokyo / translation

Interfaces cross languages and sectors.

Fashion Week, textile trade, bilingual metadata, awards and retained seasons sit in one organizational ecology.

What comparison can establish

Public organizer records reveal different emphases: transmission, time, inheritance, legitimacy, synchronization, verification and translation. They do not establish that one city is more advanced, open, sustainable or technologically sophisticated than another.

What still requires field reporting

Show-level vendors, contracts, accessibility practice, worker experience, energy and material boundaries, audience measurement, data governance, buyer conversion, archival custody and permission trails remain uneven or private. Those blanks are assignments, not invitations to speculate.
