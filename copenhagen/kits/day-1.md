## Governing question

When a show schedule has an evidence threshold, what exactly has—and has not—been verified?

## Canonical essay

Copenhagen turns participation into an evidence problem: admission, time, teardown, rights, screening, product data and institutional memory each demand a record. This chapter follows access: The decisive technology is not the door but the stack of schedule, accreditation, invitation and identity checks that precedes it.

CPHFW introduced its framework in 2020 and has required applicants to document compliance with Minimum Standards for official schedule consideration since January 2023. That earlier record fixes a point in time; it does not prove an inevitable progression toward the present.

The distinction is material. A polished interface can survive after its workers, contracts, specifications or permission trail disappear, leaving the audience with an outcome but not its operating history.

For SS27, the organizer says 34 show and presentation brands were reviewed by its show committee and a Rambøll-led sustainability committee against 19 Minimum Standards. The claim remains attached to its named source because public visibility is evidence of publication—not independent proof of every underlying practice.

Operationally, the chain runs through the organizer, accrediting body, publicist, security team and guest. Each controls a different record or decision, while the finished show encourages us to experience those decisions as a single seamless event.

Copenhagen’s governing verb is verification. It is an editorial lens, not a claim about the city’s essence: it helps test how calendars, credentials, media, labor records, commercial data and archives become legible here.

CPHFW states that its process validates submitted information but is not a certification scheme or audit; admission must not be restated as proof that a brand or product is sustainable. A future plate is therefore a documented trajectory or an explicit open question—not a forecast manufactured from a press release.
